# Nathan Adams' Quick and Dirty Portfolio

I first got interested in programming around 1995 when I learned that I could program my TI-85 calculator to do the quadratic formula so I could cheat on my math test. It's not that I couldn't do the quadratic forumula, it was just laborious. After that, I wrote a bot that ran on mIRC to trade games with other software pirates - *type !moon to see my list of warez*. Then, I got into "hacking" for a bit. I used a *war-dialer* to connect to every local modem in my area-code and record what it found. What it ultimately found was an ATM. So, I used a brute-force password cracker to guess the login info for an account on my local dial-up ISP - I assumed someone must have the login *jsmith*. Using that account, I tried an SMTP exploit to gain root access to a random server that I found in Pennsylvania. My plan was to use that system to dial out to the ATM. However, my plan was thwarted when my mom got a very serious call with questions about some suspicious activity. When I tell the story now, I say it was the FBI, but it was probably just somebody from my ISP. Regardless, it was enough to make me shred half a dozen binders of hacking references and swear off the potentially criminal activity. 

My proudest accomplishments from that time were a simple text editor that I wrote entirely in assembly language to run on the ZShell crack on the TI-85 and a simple RGB-HEX color picker app that I wrote in Visual Basic and posted on my Anglefire website. I got a ton of thank you notes for the color-picker app which, in retrospect, is what motivates me to make software. I simply enjoy creating things and then having other people like them.

After high school, I initially went to college to study software engineering. Foolishly, however, I decided that was too *nerdy* and switched my major to math (Obviously, that's where the cool kids were at). That decision stings today because what I really wanted to study was artificial intelligence. Shortly after I graduated with my math degree, I did try building a neural network in Java. I saw a documentary about learning and the brain where I learned that "neurons that fire together, wire together". I built a neural network with weighted input vectors and a very cool 3D visualization. I did not, however, understand anything about how to set the weights for the connections or training so I just had a fancy 3D neural network that spit out nonsense. Then I became a math teacher.

It wasn't until smartphones came out that I got back into software development. As soon as I discovered that anyone could develop and publish apps, I started learning how to develop for Android. That is where my portfolio begins.

## Android
### GoPuppetYourself (2015) [https://github.com/rubiconbezique/GoPuppetYourself](https://github.com/rubiconbezique/GoPuppetYourself)
The idea behind this app was for users to take a photo of something, then turn that photo into a little puppet that they could use to make silly videos. It was awesome! I learned how the basics of Android development and how to programmatically manipulate the pixels of an image to do things like remove the background.

### Mixmatic LoopPad (2016-17) [https://github.com/rubiconbezique/MixmaticLoopPadPro](https://github.com/rubiconbezique/MixmaticLoopPadPro)
This was a DJ app inspired by the Ableton Launchpad. The idea was that users could assign samples to a grid of buttons and then use those buttons to mix the samples together to create music. This was my first professional level product. I partnered with a company to supply the samples which users could then purchase through the app. It had some really cool features. You could import an mp3 from your own library and then use the tools I built into the app to automatically detect beats and slice the song into samples that could be remixed. I learned a ton about working with audio and signal processing as well as creating custom android View components. Unfortunately, a major life event derailed this project and I never managed to see it to its full potential. By the time I was able to get back to it, the market was flooded with DJ apps. I did manage to get it up to over 10,000 installs, however.

### Arithmos (2016) [https://github.com/rubiconbezique/Arithmos](https://github.com/rubiconbezique/Arithmos)
This was a math game based on word search style games. I noticed that my math students really struggled with the order of operations. So, I made this game to help them practice in a way that was more fun. You would have a list of target numbers to search for. Then, you had to select a sequence of numbers and choose the operations that would result in the one of the target numbers. I learned a lot about game design and working with the Google Play Games API. Ultimately, Android wasn't the right platform for this game. I did get a few thousand installs, but it wasn't practical for my own students. If I were to remake something like this again, I would build it as a website.

## AI & Machine Learning

### Metatrader 4 Trading Bots (2017) *Code not available*
These were algorithmic FOREX currency trading bots I wrote in MQL4 to trade currency pairs with Metatrader 4. I came upon this while teaching a financial math class. MQL4 is an interpretted superset of C++. Building these projects I learned about technical analysis of securities charts and became familiar with using a C++ style language such as working with pointers and managing memory. They actually worked surprisingly well. I built one that earned a 50% ROI after a month! Unfortunately, I only invested $1k and at one point, an unhandled edge case resulted in the bot opening dozens of trades that would immediately close over night and losing all but $50 in profit $1 at a time.

### Java Aritificial Neural Network Implementation (2020) *Code not available*
The COVID lockdown was my first year teaching computer science as a CTE program. While researching for my new CS program, I came across a [Python tutorial](https://enlight.nyc/projects/neural-network) using numpy to implement a simple artificial neural network. That tutorial re-ignited my interest in artificial neural networks and I began researching the concepts and math behind them. I learned about [activation functions](https://towardsdatascience.com/activation-functions-in-neural-networks-how-to-choose-the-right-one-cb20414c04e5/) and [back propagation](https://codesachin.wordpress.com/2015/12/06/backpropagation-for-dummies/) and managed to make a functional, object-based, multilayer perceptron artificial neural network in Java. 

### MQL4 Deep-Q Learning Artificial Neural Network Implementation (2020) *Code not available*
Once I had a functional ANN that I had built myself, I immediately thought about using it with my currency trading bots, and I just so happened upon [this tutorial for a ANN in MQL4](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/7447#para53). The structure was essentially the same as my Java implementation, so I rebuilt and extended it in MQL4 using that tutorial as a guide. After a few hours of manually creating training data, I concluded that a simple MLP was just not practical. Somewhere along the way, I stumbled upon the idea of reinforcement learning. After some more research, I decided to build a Deep-Q learning algorithm on top of my MQL4 MLP implementation. Many [articles](https://mlq.ai/guides/deep-reinforcement-learning-q-learning/) and [tutorials](https://towardsdatascience.com/deep-q-learning-tutorial-mindqn-2a4c855abffc/) later, I had built a DQN. It's actually fairly simple in concept. And it did learn. However, my MLP ANN implementation was just too simplistic. Even though I had added batching, tested a variety of replay buffer strategies, and tried dozens of different reward functions, the model just didn't learn a winning strategy. Plus, my code had become such a mess from all the quick tests and hasty implementations, it became untenable and I abandoned the project.
